1. The environments

RoomsGridWorld — for dynamic programming

. . . . . . G          S  start (state 35)
. # # . . # .          G  goal,   +10, terminal
. . . . # . .          H  hazard,  -8, terminal
. # . . H . .          #  wall
. # . # # . .
S . . . . . .          every move: -1

6 × 7 grid, 42 states, γ = 0.95. Moving into a wall or off the border leaves you where you are and still costs −1.

CliffWalk — for TD control

. . . . . . . . . .    S  start (state 40)
. . . . . . . . . .    G  goal, terminal
. . . . . . . . . .    C  cliff: −75 and you are returned to S,
. . . . . . . . . .       but the episode does NOT end
S C C C C C C C C G
                       every move: -1

5 × 10 grid, 50 states, γ = 0.99.

Actions in both: 0 = up, 1 = right, 2 = down, 3 = left. States are row-major, state = row * n_cols + col. The model is exposed as env.P[s][a] -> [(prob, next_state, reward, terminated)].

2. Conventions — read this before you write code

Your numeric answers are checked against a reference implementation. These conventions decide the numbers, so follow them exactly.

Rule
Policy evaluation Synchronous: read from V_k, write into a separate V_{k+1}. Not in-place
Initialisation V = 0 everywhere, including terminal states
Terminal states V stays 0; no updates
Convergence Stop when max|V_{k+1} − V_k| < 1e-12
Sweep counting One sweep = one full pass over all states. The first pass is sweep 1
argmax ties Break toward the lowest action index (up < right < down < left)
Policy iteration init Deterministic policy: action 0 (up) in every state
Policy iteration counting One round = one evaluation-to-convergence plus one improvement. Count the final round in which the policy does not change

An in-place sweep or a different tie-break gives different numbers. That is not a rounding difference; it is a different algorithm.

Two of these are load-bearing here, not hypothetical. In-place evaluation changes the 10-sweep values noticeably. And at least one of the probe states below has two genuinely optimal actions, so the tie-break rule decides the answer you report — both actions are correct policy, but only one matches the reference.

3. What to implement

Dynamic programming (RoomsGridWorld)

  • Iterative policy evaluation for the uniform random policy
  • Policy iteration
  • Value iteration

TD control (CliffWalk)

  • ε-greedy action selection
  • SARSA
  • Q-learning

Use α = 0.5, ε = 0.1, 3000 episodes, and a 400-step cap per episode. Run at least 5 seeds. Keep training behaviour and greedy evaluation separate: during training you act ε-greedily; during evaluation you act greedily and do not learn.

Probe states

Label RoomsGridWorld CliffWalk

Definitions for the TD entries

  • *_training_return — mean undiscounted episode return over the last 500 training episodes, averaged over your seeds
  • *_greedy_return — undiscounted return of the final greedy policy from the start state, averaged over seeds
  • *_highest_row — the smallest row index the greedy path visits, averaged over seeds. Row 0 is the top row, row 4 is the cliff row. This number is what separates the two algorithms

4.4 Report — 3 to 4 pages

PDF only. Answer these four questions with your own figures and numbers.

  1. Compare the convergence and the final policies of policy iteration and value iteration. Do they reach the same policy? How many sweeps does each need, and why do the counts differ?
  2. Explain the difference between the paths SARSA and Q-learning learn. Use *_highest_row to make the claim concrete.
  3. Analyse the effect of ε on exploration, training return and final greedy evaluation. Run at least three values of ε.
  4. Q-learning gets a worse training return than SARSA, but a better greedy evaluation. Explain why both facts are true at once, and what that says about what each algorithm is estimating.

6. Notes

Sanity checks you can run yourself. Policy iteration and value iteration must arrive at the same optimal policy. Your V_optimal for CliffWalk should decrease smoothly with distance from the goal. If the greedy policy from your Q ever fails to reach the goal, you have a bug — the discount factor was chosen so this cannot happen at convergence.

AI coding assistants are permitted, under the conditions in the setup guide: disclose their use in your README, take responsibility for correctness, and be prepared to explain any line. Note that these environments are not the textbook ones — an implementation recalled from memory rather than derived from env.P will produce confidently wrong numbers.

If something looks broken in pa1_envs.py, report it rather than editing it. Corrections will be distributed to everyone.
